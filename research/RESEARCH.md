## Original game writing — invented, not real quotes

The stronger comic mechanism is a goal that becomes embarrassing when achieved. Let an NPC remember what the player asked for; give them a beat of silence before the reversal.

| Quest | Original Hinglish exchange | Playable consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Friend Finder | Player: “Bhai, group photo mein teen aaye the. Return mein bhi teen hi chahiye.” Friend on phone: “Hum toh chai pe hain. Tu kahan hai?” | Reunite a moving group; a premature reunion choice sends the player to the wrong meeting point. |
| Mummy Calling | “Sir, bas itna bol dena: revision chal raha hai.” Officer: “Haan, civics ka practical.” | A timed conversation with inconsistent alibis; wrong subject produces a follow-up question. This is fiction inspired by an unverified lead. |
| Complimentary Confusion | “Uncle, aapka serious face bhi friendly lag raha hai.” Officer: “Beta, compliment se queue fast nahi hoti.” | Charm changes banter and crowd mood; it does not magically remove every obstacle. Unverified-lead fiction. |
| Weather Department | “Paani ka arrangement hai?” Volunteer: “Bottle wala. Expectations neeche.” | Deliver water to NPCs, coordinate cups and shade; payoff is a very small misting fan. |
| Attendance Optional | “Protest mein attendance nahi lagti.” Friend: “Mummy ko location bhejni padti hai.” | Find a quiet spot for a call while keeping track of friends. |
| Detain-Me Regret | “Mere dost andar hain, main bhi aaun?” Beat. “Update: mere dost bahar hain. Request withdraw.” | Explicitly fictional farce adapted from reporting; show friends visibly changing position so the reversal is earned. |

Use local speech rhythms and interruptions rather than endless slang. Each quest needs a setup, a reversible player mistake, an NPC reaction, and a payoff that changes something visible. Keep source notes separate from invented dialogue. Prefer generic characters to recreating identifiable private protesters.
