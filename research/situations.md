# The 20 best "build this" situations

Ranked for fun: how quickly a player gets it, how visible the payoff is, and how well it feeds the day's escalation. Each one traces back to entries in `research/reels/` (ids in brackets). **Real** means what a source reports. **Game** means our adaptation, invented on top. The cast comes from `dist/loop/content.js`.

Two cautions for writers:
- Patna moments (water cannon, rope, anthem) happened in Bihar. Use them as fiction set at our composite Jantar Mantar / CP. Never present them as Delhi fact.
- No source read in this pass heard any audio. Lines marked "quoted" are as the publisher printed them, and three outlets printed the tear-gas line three different ways.

---

## 1. The bus that can't leave
`[bus-key-snatch] [police-push-bus]`
- **Real:** On 4 Oct a woman took the key of a police bus loaded with detainees and ran. The bus couldn't move, and a second clip shows police pushing it. Comments split between "Absolute cinema" and "Funny huh? In what logic?"
- **Player notices:** Iqbal leaves the key on the dashboard hook while he argues with a constable about parking (Asha's rumour: "chabi hook pe chhod dete hain").
- **Can try:** Take the key in plain sight or while he's distracted. Toss it to a crew member. Hide it in the chai stall. Give it back for a favour.
- **World reacts:** The bus dies mid-route. Iqbal searches his pockets, then everyone else's. If someone saw you, the nearest constable runs at you. If you weren't seen, Rao on the wireless orders "dhakka lagao" and four constables push the bus while the crowd counts the metres. Detainees eye the rear door.
- **Payoff:** The bus is stuck in the road, the detainees walk off, and Meera's clip is captioned "So, this is the one who took the key and ran." The comments are split: aura up with friends, a trust drop with Iqbal, heat with police.
- **Cast:** player, Iqbal, Sharma, Meera, Rao (voice).

## 2. "Sir, mujhe bhi detain kar lo"
`[detain-me-too] [dosti-over-detention]`
- **Real:** A man begged to be detained to stay with two friends. Police refused, he insisted, and they were amused and took him in the front door just as his friends slipped out the back.
- **Player notices:** Nikhil and Kabir are on the detention bus. Nikhil texts "bhai andar aa ja, akela lag raha hai."
- **Can try:** Ask Sharma to detain you. Keep insisting through two refusals. Or go round the back.
- **World reacts:** Sharma refuses, then sighs and lets you up the front steps. As you sit down, the camera shows Nikhil and Kabir climbing out the rear door. Pause. Then: "Sir… ek update hai."
- **Payoff:** A goal reversal on camera. The player now has to talk their way off a bus they begged to board, and the request is remembered: Sharma later says "Tum wahi ho na, dost wale?"
- **Cast:** player, Nikhil, Kabir, Sharma.

## 3. The weather department
`[patna-water-cannon-dance] [tear-gas-arrangement]`
- **Real (Patna):** In the heat, students asked police why they weren't using the water cannon and whether "tear gas ka intezaam" had been made. The cannon sprayed and they danced. Comments said "Ye protest ho raha hai ya Holi ki party" and "बिहार गजब है".
- **Player notices:** It's 12:30, everyone is wilting, and the white-and-blue Vajra truck is parked idle.
- **Can try:** Ask the crew politely for "thoda paani". Get Kabir to ask Sharma, in a customer-service voice, whether tear gas is "available today". Get Ravi to put a song on.
- **World reacts:** In the morning they laugh it off ("Sir, abhi permission nahi hai"). The request is remembered. When the cannon really fires at 15:45, the crowd you primed doesn't scatter: they dance.
- **Payoff:** `cannon-dance`. Intensity drops, the RAF line hesitates, and the reel is captioned "protest hai ya Holi party". Sana yells about wet phones.
- **Cast:** Kabir, Sharma, Rao, Ravi, Sana.

## 4. Subway Surfer IRL
`[subway-surfer-irl] [chase-bollywood-edits] [parliament-doorbell]`
- **Real:** A self-filmed chase was captioned "Subway Surfer IRL at Jantar Mantar Protest" (reported at 54.8M views). Chases were recut to Chak De, Bhag Milkha, Bhag DK Bose and Gangnam Style. Captions included "Parliament ki doorbell baja ke bhag gaya!" and "Thanks for the cardio, Delhi Police!"
- **Player notices:** A constable starts running at you, and the music hard-cuts to a looping runner track.
- **Can try:** Weave through the chai stall, under barricades, through a crowd of 6+. Or do a "ding-dong-ditch": tap the Parliament Street barricade and sprint.
- **World reacts:** The constable gets winded, a second one joins, and bystanders part or block depending on your aura.
- **Payoff:** Meera offers three edits: sports anthem, runner game, K-pop. Each gets its own caption ("Bro thought he can outrun me"), and later each draws a different crowd reaction. The ending card reads "You ran 3.2 km. Delhi Police thanks you for the cardio."
- **Cast:** player, Meera, Kabir.

## 5. Mummy is calling (civics ka practical)
`[tuition-teacher-call]` (owner lead, unverified: play as fiction)
- **Real:** No source found. The closest real energy is the reel culture above and the "GRWM to get detained" trend.
- **Player notices:** At 10:40 Kabir's phone buzzes and his voice drops two registers: "Bhai, mummy. Tuition mein hona chahiye tha."
- **Can try:** Hand the phone to Sharma. Pretend to be the teacher yourself. Tell Kabir to come clean. Film it.
- **World reacts:** A bored Sharma declines. An amused one takes the phone: "Haan ji, civics ka practical chal raha hai." Mummy asks a follow-up question about the syllabus.
- **Payoff:** Sharma moves to `soft`. Callback: at 17:00 mummy calls again and quotes the subject you made up.
- **Cast:** Kabir, Sharma, player.

## 6. The pookie cop
`[pookie-cop] [cute-uncle]`
- **Real:** A Mumbai constable went viral as the "pookie cop" after a creator's reel set to a Lana Del Rey audio. Shown his own clip, he blushed. The trend spread to police at many sites.
- **Player notices:** Meera is lining up a "pookie" bit with her ring light, and Sharma is pretending not to see.
- **Can try:** Call Sharma "pookie" or "uncle, aap cute ho" with the camera on or off. Do it once, or three times.
- **World reacts:** With the camera on, he holds a stern face for a beat, then fails. Off camera, he grins and says "duty pe hoon." The third push makes him `annoyed`.
- **Payoff:** At 13:00 Sharma shows *you* his own viral reel on his phone. Later he lets your crew through the barricade.
- **Cast:** Meera, Sharma.

## 7. "Sir ji, nashta hoga?"
`[gujarat-nashta] [imran-van-selfie]`
- **Real:** Detained students in Gujarat asked "Sir ji, nashta hoga?" and the reply was "Chai nashta sab hoga!" They played Ludo, chanted "We love you, Gujarat Police!", and captioned it "10/10 Service". In Mumbai an actor posted a van selfie: "Apparently I'm going for a drive."
- **Player notices:** You're on the detention bus. It's hot, and the guard is eating.
- **Can try:** Ask for breakfast. Start Ludo on the phone. Take a barred-window selfie. Start a "Resign, resign!" chant.
- **World reacts:** A soft Sharma brings samosas and the bus turns into a party. An annoyed one writes your name on the list.
- **Payoff:** Meera's "detention vlog" clip. Your mood decides whether its caption reads brave or smug.
- **Cast:** Sharma, Kabir, Meera.

## 8. A hand on the bonnet
`[rhiya-ahir-van] [chembur-van-latch]`
- **Real:** In Mumbai a woman asked a van crew for their detention paperwork, then stood in front of the van for about 40 minutes until the detainees were released. In Chembur a man simply opened a van's latch and detainees jumped out while a woman clapped.
- **Player notices:** The detention bus is pulling out with Mira inside.
- **Can try:** Stand in front of it. Rally the crew to put hands on the bonnet. Ask Iqbal "kagaz hai?". Sneak to the rear latch while the guard chats.
- **World reacts:** Iqbal revs and you flinch (the screen shakes). Each crew member who joins adds a hand. With enough hands Iqbal kills the engine. Or the latch opens and detainees trickle out one at a time.
- **Payoff:** Mira walks free, and the photo of your hand on the bus becomes the evening's image. Heat high, help high.
- **Cast:** player, Iqbal, Mira, crew.

## 9. Tug of war with the police rope
`[patna-rope-tug-of-war] [patna-barricade-swing]`
- **Real (Patna):** Police brought a rope to hold the crowd back. Students grabbed the other end and played tug of war, and others used a barricade as a swing.
- **Player notices:** At 14:00 constables stretch a thick rope across the road, and its slack end lies on the tarmac.
- **Can try:** Pick up the end. Shout for the crew.
- **World reacts:** A tug of war breaks out. Constables dig in, and Rao on the wireless says "rassi chhodo mat."
- **Payoff:** Win and the line moves back two metres while the crowd roars. Lose and the crew falls in a heap at the barricade. Either way it makes a clip.
- **Cast:** player, Kabir, crew, Sharma.

## 10. The anthem freezes the line
`[patna-anthem-halt]`
- **Real (Patna):** A shield-and-lathi column was advancing. The crowd sang the national anthem, and the officers halted, stood erect and sang along.
- **Player notices:** It's the charge. The RAF line steps forward and Mira is at the mic.
- **Can try:** Tell Mira to start the anthem. Start it yourself. Get Ravi to put it on the speaker.
- **World reacts:** If enough people join within a few seconds, the line freezes at attention. When the anthem ends there's an awkward silence.
- **Payoff:** The charge is delayed, `chargeAt` slips, and the ending card says why.
- **Cast:** Mira, Ravi.

## 11. Breakfast for the hungry constables

## 12. "Likhit mein do"

- **Player notices:** Two constables come for Mira at the stall at 10:30.

## 13. Gandhiji, where are you running?
`[gandhi-lookalike-chase] [superhero-cosplay]`
- **Real:** On 10 Oct near Palika Bazaar, a Gandhi lookalike ran from police. His stick fell and he kept running while a voice asked "Gandhiji, kahan bhaag rahe ho?" Elsewhere people dressed as Batman, Spider-Man and "Dora the Protester".
- **Player notices:** There's a costume crate at the volunteer tent.
- **Can try:** Dress Kabir up, dress yourself up, or dress Nikhil up so he's easier to find.
- **World reacts:** Everyone photographs the costume. When the chase starts, the stick drops in the road.
- **Payoff:** Pick up the stick, return it, or keep it. Costumed people are recognised by every constable later, which is good for finding friends and bad for heat.
- **Cast:** Kabir, Nikhil.

## 14. The white-shirt men and the stone truck (provocateurs)
`[men-without-nameplates] [stone-truck] [patna-party-clash]`
- **Real:** On 20 July, videos showed men in jeans and white shirts swinging police lathis. Police HQ then ordered uniform-only duty. The movement's founder asked who parked a truck of stones at the site, and police said it was a municipal truck. Police said facial recognition found 2,873 people with records. Both sides blame outsiders.
- **Player notices:** From 14:00 a municipal dumper of rubble sits next to the RUP SUV. Three men have new shoes, an earpiece and a helmet in a bag. Later, some lathi-swingers have no nameplate.
- **Can try:** Film the truck. Ask Dev who called it. Report the men to a soft Sharma. Expose them on Zoya's stream.
- **World reacts:** If nothing is done, the first stone comes off that truck at 15:30. Exposed on camera, the men run to the SUV.
- **Payoff:** Intensity and target change. The evening ticker either blames "outsiders" or names the SUV, depending on whose clip is out first.
- **Cast:** Dev, Zoya, Rao, Sharma.

## 20. The rumour that outruns the facts
`[counter-mobilisation-posts] [fakes-and-deepfakes]`
- **Real:** Before 10 Oct, posts claimed "5 lakh" to "10 lakh" Hindutva workers were coming ("Bajrang Dal is moving!"), and old saffron-scarf footage recirculated. In July a deepfake showed a DCP "resigning", and police alleged "Pakistan-based handlers".

- **Can try:** Go and look, then film the real size (six bikes and a megaphone). Forward the rumour. Ask Zoya.

- **Payoff:** The crowd size and the police posture visibly change on who the player believed.
- **Cast:** Zoya, Nikhil, Rao.

## Reference game: mantarjantar.com ("Jantar Mantar Chalo!")

Sources: [mantarjantar.com](https://mantarjantar.com/) (page text read 2026-10-20; the game itself was not played this pass), [bugswriter.com/jantar-mantar-game](https://bugswriter.com/jantar-mantar-game/), [ThePrint feature](https://theprint.in/feature/jantar-mantar-chalo-the-video-game-where-protesters-dodge-cops-tear-gas/3072466/), [Telangana Today round-up](https://telanganatoday.com/from-tear-gas-to-water-cannons-delhi-protest-scenes-recreated-in-video-games), and the 16 Oct offline play notes in `live-research-2026-10-16.json`.

**What it is.** A one-person, AI-assisted multiplayer browser game (plus an Android APK) by Suraj Kushwah (bugswriter), built in about two nights. Players pick Andolankari or Dilli Police.
- Protesters walk into people to grow a *kafila* and push barricades.
- Police swing lathis, throw tear gas and "lift leaders onto the bus".
- On the map: golgappas restore stamina, a gamchha dodges CCTV, and a media house is 50/50 "independent" (people join) or "Godi media" (people leave).
- Events: DMRC gate closures, internet outages, and detention to Narela or the Kapashera border.
- Reported 1,500 to 2,000 concurrent players at peak (ThePrint, Telangana Today).

**What it does well, and what we take:**
- **Verbs you understand in one second.** The buttons are NAARA, LIFT, GAS and DASH. Hold to push a barricade. Hold right-click to run, and the kafila runs too. Our slice verbs should be just as legible.
- **A satirical ticker** that reinterprets play while it continues (seen in the 16 Oct offline session). We take the idea for the phone ticker and make it fallible: it reports what was filmed, not what happened.
- **Local texture as mechanics.** Golgappas, gamchha vs CCTV, Narela detention and the 50/50 media house each carry a joke and a rule at once. Our objects should too (Asha's chai, the bus key, the pankha).
- **Graceful hardware fallback.** An automatic "fast graphics" switch (Q toggles graphics). We need the same: a visible performance preset with the same rules.
- **Tone discipline.** The creator says "It's just chaos, presented as a game" and won't "tell anyone what to think". It has a satire disclaimer that all shops and brands are fictional, and a joke donation button labelled "❤️ Foreign Funding". The labels are part of the comedy.
- **Players retell accidents.** Launch-reel comments ("detained while dancing") show that combinable systems create anecdotes.

**What we should not copy:**
- **Score-and-swarm structure.** It's a Slither-style crowd-grow loop with points. We are a character day with memory. Our people need names, wants and callbacks, not bodies to absorb.
- **The binary media coin-flip.** "Godi vs independent" as a 50/50 is a good gag but flat as a model. Our Zoya, Meera and the police PR feed should each have motives, and a caption should change *who* reacts later.
- **Real names and AI art.** It uses real places and, in sibling games, real people (Jantar Mantar to Jail uses real CJP leaders, and another game taps the CEC on the head). Our rule is fictional stand-ins (RUP, DRS, LJM) and composite characters. Its art is AI-assisted. Our audio rule is real reel voices and published music, no generated music or TTS.
- **Police as pure obstacle.** Its police role is "break kafilas". Our police must be people: Sharma can be soft, Rao is under pressure, and the RAF follow orders.
- **Multiplayer first.** Not our scope.

Other protest games from the same weeks, for awareness: *Bhaag Neha Bhaag* (an endless runner after an AISA leader's repeated attempts to reach the site; about 2,700 players) and *Jantar Mantar to Jail* (collect "Democracy" coins), both by Wizloop Studios, plus an untitled game where you tap the CEC on the head.
