# Feel and look rubric

## Look (judge from screenshots)

Does this look like a real game a person would screenshot and post, or like a cheap one? Compare with what a
browser game can do today (well-lit Three.js scenes with photo textures, dense crowds, real shop fronts).

1. Placeholder blocks and empty ground; looks like a prototype.
2. Some real assets, but large flat or empty areas.
3. Consistent and lit, but sparse and toy-like.
4. Dense, textured and lit; looks like a small commercial game.
5. Looks like a big-studio game at a glance.

## Hands (judge by playing, or from a video)

Does moving, running, dodging and getting chased feel good? Look for: instant response to input, a chase that
needs decisions (where to cut, when to hide, what to vault), near misses, a camera and music that kick in, a funny
and quick capture, and wanting to go again.

1. Moving is just getting from A to B; nothing to master.
2. A chase exists but holding one key wins it.
3. Some decisions in a chase; it is tense once or twice.
4. Several short games that each feel good and are different.
5. You keep playing the low-level game for its own sake, like Subway Surfers.

Answer with JSON: score (1-5, halves allowed), why (two sentences), fix (the single change that would raise the
score most).
